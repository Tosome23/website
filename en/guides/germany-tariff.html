Bought Something From Korea?
Here's the EU's New €3 Flat Duty

If a Korean seller ships your order to Germany or any other EU country, and the parcel is worth €150 or less, you'll now be charged a flat €3 customs duty per distinct item type — a rule that took effect July 1, 2026. VAT was already charged on every EU-bound order regardless of value since 2021, and that continues on top of this new duty.

🕒 Last updated: August 31, 2026

What's new here, and what was already true

VAT has applied to every parcel entering the EU regardless of value since July 2021, when the old €22 VAT-free threshold was scrapped. What's new is customs duty: parcels under €150 used to be duty-free entirely. The European Commission found that this threshold was being exploited by overseas sellers through under-declared values and artificially split shipments, and introduced a temporary flat customs duty of €3 per item, effective July 1, 2026[1][2].

Timeline

DateWhat happened
Jul 2021EU e-commerce VAT reform — €22 VAT exemption scrapped, VAT now applies to every order regardless of value
Jul 1, 2026New €3 flat customs duty per item type introduced for parcels of €150 or less
Jul 1, 2028 (planned)This temporary measure is due to end, transitioning to the standard duty regime

The €3 charge is per item type, not per parcel

If your parcel contains several distinct customs classifications (HS lines), the €3 charge is multiplied by however many different item types are inside. Five identical T-shirts in one box is €3 total. One T-shirt plus one watch — two different item types — is €6.

What's in the parcelFlat duty charged
Multiples of the same item (e.g. 5 T-shirts)€3
2 different item types (e.g. T-shirt + watch)€6
3 different item types€9

This flat duty is separate from VAT, so your real total is roughly: (item price × your country's VAT rate) + (number of item types × €3).

Korea-EU FTA products are exempt from this specific charge

✅ Products that qualify for preferential treatment under the Korea-EU free trade agreement are exempt from this new €3 flat duty — but only if the seller has the correct origin documentation attached to the shipment. If that paperwork is missing, the item is charged the standard €3 like anything else, even if it would otherwise have qualified.

If your order has a lot of different items in it

⚠️ Orders with many different kinds of products in one box add up fast under this rule. This flat duty is paid by the IOSS-registered party, importer, or seller/indirect agent — not collected directly from the buyer at the door in most setups — so it may already be reflected in your checkout price rather than billed separately on delivery. Worth confirming with the seller if you're placing a large mixed order.
