Bought Something From Korea?
GST Still Applies, Even Under AUD 1,000

Even if your order from a Korean seller is worth less than AUD 1,000, Australia still charges 10% GST on it — the common belief that "under AUD 1,000 is tax-free" is not accurate. What's actually waived below that threshold is customs duty and the requirement for a formal import declaration, not GST itself.

🕒 Last updated: August 31, 2026

Why people think it's tax-free — and why it isn't

Since July 1, 2018, Australia's Low Value Imported Goods GST regime has required overseas sellers whose annual sales into Australia exceed AUD 75,000 to register for GST[1]. Registered sellers collect 10% GST at the point of sale — when you pay for the item — rather than at the border. So the tax hasn't disappeared; it's just being collected earlier, at checkout instead of at customs. If you didn't notice GST on your receipt, check whether it was already folded into the displayed price.

GST above AUD 1,000 is calculated as (customs value + duty + freight/insurance) × 10%[2]. For example, an AUD 2,000 item with AUD 200 freight and a 5% duty rate: duty = AUD 100, GST = (2,000+100+200) × 10% = AUD 230.

Don't take "under AUD 1,000, no tax" at face value

⚠️ GST applies either way — the only real question is whether it was already collected at checkout or will be collected when the parcel arrives. If you shop repeatedly from a Korean seller and never see GST registration mentioned, it's worth asking whether they're an ATO-registered seller (required once they cross AUD 75,000/year in Australian sales) — unregistered sellers can leave you with an unexpected GST bill at the border. Alcohol and tobacco always carry extra duty and excise (WET, LCT, etc.) regardless of value.

If you're ordering something over AUD 1,000

✅ Once your order passes AUD 1,000, expect a formal import declaration and an Import Processing Charge on top of duty and GST — factor that into the total cost before you buy. Make sure the declared customs value (item price + freight + insurance) is accurate, since GST is calculated from that combined figure, not just the item price.
