Bought Something From Korea?
Here's What Decides Your China Import Tax

If a Korean seller is shipping your order to China, what you pay depends almost entirely on whether that seller uses a Cross-Border E-Commerce (CBEC, 跨境电商) channel. Inside that channel, orders under 5,000 RMB (and 26,000 RMB per year, per buyer) pay zero customs duty and only 70% of the normal VAT and consumption tax. Cross that limit, and the order is taxed in full as ordinary trade.

🕒 Last updated: August 31, 2026

What the CBEC channel is, and why it matters to you

China introduced its Cross-Border E-Commerce (CBEC) retail import tax policy in 2016, originally with a 2,000 RMB per-order / 20,000 RMB annual limit[1]. The rule was revised in 2018 and took effect January 1, 2019 with the current, higher limits: 5,000 RMB per order and 26,000 RMB per buyer per year[2]. Those are still the numbers in force as of 2026. If your Korean seller registered their store to sell through this channel, your order is taxed under these much friendlier terms — if not, it's treated as a normal commercial import from the moment it lands.

How the tax is actually calculated

Inside the CBEC limit, you pay zero customs duty, and VAT plus any consumption tax is charged at just 70% of the normal rate. So for a product with a standard 13% VAT rate, you're effectively paying about 13% × 70% ≈ 9.1%.

CBEC (within limit)0% duty + (VAT + consumption tax) × 70%The best outcome for buyers, if the seller supports it
CBEC limit exceededFull duty + 13% VAT + consumption tax (if applicable)Reprocessed as an ordinary trade import
Personal parcel (gift)China's parcel tax (行邮税) — small amounts are effectively duty-freeRepeated shipments can be reclassified as commercial

The same item can be treated differently depending on how it's packaged, how often it's shipped, and what the recipient's information looks like to customs — this is a judgment call made at the border, not a fixed rule per product.

What to check if your package gets held up

⚠️ If you're a repeat buyer from the same Korean store and your parcels start getting delayed, it may be because customs is treating what looks like frequent "gift" shipments as disguised commercial trade. This is a seller-side registration issue, not something you can fix as the buyer — worth flagging to the seller if it happens more than once. Branded items, even genuine ones, can also be asked to prove authenticity, which can add delay.

If you buy regularly from the same Korean store

✅ If you expect to place larger or more frequent orders that will exceed the CBEC limits, ask your seller whether they can prepare proper ordinary-trade documentation from the start — it avoids repeated hold-ups. Products genuinely made in Korea can also often qualify for reduced rates under the China–Korea FTA if the seller provides a certificate of origin, which can meaningfully lower what you owe on larger orders.
