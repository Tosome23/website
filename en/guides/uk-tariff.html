Bought Something From Korea?
The UK's £135 Rule Only Covers Duty — Not VAT

If a Korean seller ships your order to the UK and it's worth £135 or less, you won't be charged customs duty — but VAT (20%) still applies in most cases. "Under £135 means tax-free" is a common assumption, but it only describes what happens to duty, not VAT.

🕒 Last updated: August 31, 2026

£135 splits duty and VAT differently

Per the UK government's official guidance (GOV.UK), goods valued at £135 or below aren't charged customs duty[1]. VAT is calculated separately: for an ordinary online purchase, the seller has often already included VAT in the price you paid at checkout, so you owe nothing further — but if they haven't, the courier will collect VAT from you when the parcel clears customs[1].

Gifts are treated differently

⚠️ The UK has a separate, lower VAT-free threshold specifically for genuine gifts: £39[2]. To qualify, four conditions all need to be true: (1) the sender must be a private individual located outside the UK, (2) the recipient must also be a private individual, (3) no one in the UK paid for it, and (4) it must be occasional in nature — think a birthday or Christmas present, not a routine purchase[2]. Something you bought yourself from an online store and had shipped directly to you does not qualify as a "gift," even if it's marked as one.

Above £135, VAT is calculated at 20% of the item price plus shipping, packaging, insurance, and duty combined — and the courier (DHL, FedEx, UPS, etc.) typically pays this on your behalf first, then bills you before or at delivery.

A bigger change is coming October 2028

In its November 2025 budget announcement, the UK government confirmed it will abolish the £135 duty exemption entirely on October 1, 2028 — earlier than the March 2029 date originally floated[3]. The details of what replaces it (a flat per-item fee, a different VAT collection method, or something else) are still under consultation as of August 2026 and haven't been finalized[3].

Don't assume £135 means no cost at all

⚠️ "Under £135 means tax-free" isn't accurate — duty is waived, but VAT usually still applies, and whether you owe anything extra at delivery depends on whether the seller already collected it at checkout. Declaring a genuine commercial purchase as a "gift" to try to dodge tax can cause delays or have the parcel held at the border.

What to check before you buy

✅ Check whether your Korean seller is UK VAT-registered and whether VAT is already included in the checkout price — that's the single biggest factor in whether you'll be asked for more money when the parcel arrives. An accurate invoice with the real purchase price also matters for both the £135 duty check and how smoothly the parcel clears.
