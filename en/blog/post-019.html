Dangerous Goods Packing
UN-Certified Containers & 2026 Standards

Packing matters as much as paperwork when it comes to dangerous goods. Get the packing wrong, and even perfect documentation won't stop the airline from rejecting the shipment or having it returned at the destination. Here's everything: UN-certified container types and how to read the codes, packing group requirements, inner/outer packing rules, labeling and marking regulations, and Limited Quantity packing conditions — organized under 2026 air transport standards.

1. Why Packing Matters — Common Reasons Shipments Get Returned

In dangerous goods transport, poor packing isn't just a matter of damage. It leads to airline rejection, seizure at destination customs, or the entire shipment being returned. Here are the most common causes.

🚫 Common reasons shipments are returned for poor packing:
① Not using a UN-certified container (using a plain cardboard box instead)
② No UN number or warning label on the outer packaging
③ Insufficient insulation or waterproofing on the inner packaging
④ Lithium batteries packed above 30% state of charge
⑤ Mixing dangerous goods with general cargo in the same package

Packing standards vary by hazard class and packing group (PG). Always confirm the packing requirements for your specific item before shipping.

2. What Is a UN-Certified Container? — Types and How to Read the Codes

A UN-certified container is packaging that meets UN standards for safely containing dangerous goods. The UN certification mark and code are printed on the outer packaging so it can be verified.

💡 How to read a UN code: example 4G/X5/S/26/KR/AA1234
4G = fiberboard box / X = highest packing group rating (approved for PG I) / 5 = maximum gross weight (kg) / S = for solids / 26 = year of manufacture / KR = country / AA1234 = manufacturer certification number

4. Inner and Outer Packing Requirements — Air Transport Standards

Dangerous goods packing is composed of two layers: inner packaging (primary packaging) and outer packaging. Each layer has its own requirements.

01
Inner packaging — the container in direct contact
This is the first container in direct contact with the dangerous good. Chemical resistance of the material and leak-proof sealing are key.
Liquids: fill to 95% of container volume or below (headspace for thermal expansion) Batteries: discharge to 30% SOC or below + individual insulation Double-sealed caps/stoppers to prevent leaks — accounting for low cabin pressure Wrap the inner container in absorbent material (vermiculite, absorbent paper, etc.)
02
Outer packaging — UN-certified containers
This is the final outer container holding the inner packaging. Depending on the PG, you must use a UN-certified X/Y/Z grade container or higher, as specified.
Confirm the UN certification mark and code are printed on the outer packaging Fill empty space with cushioning material (bubble wrap, foam, etc.) to prevent shifting Seal and reinforce joints with tape
03
No mixed packing of incompatible dangerous goods
Dangerous goods that could react with each other cannot go in the same outer package. Mixing dangerous goods with general cargo may also be restricted.
Oxidizers (Class 5) and flammable substances (Class 3) cannot be mixed Avoid packing general cargo like documents or clothing in the same box

6. Limited Quantity Packing Exemption Conditions

A Limited Quantity (LQ) exemption applies relaxed packing and documentation rules once a dangerous good's inner packaging volume falls under a certain threshold. All conditions must be met precisely to qualify.

①
Meet the inner packaging volume threshold
IATA sets a maximum volume per inner package for each item under the LQ rules. Example: for Class 3 flammable liquid, PG II — inner packaging must be 1L or below.
②
Total weight of 30kg or below per package
If the total weight per outer package exceeds 30kg, it no longer qualifies for LQ treatment.
③
Attach the LQ diamond mark to the outer packaging
A white background, black-bordered diamond mark must be affixed to the outer packaging. Minimum size is 100×100mm.
④
Confirm items ineligible for LQ
Not every dangerous good qualifies for LQ treatment. Some items, such as Class 1 (explosives) and Class 7 (radioactive materials), cannot use the LQ exemption.
✅ Benefits of LQ eligibility: DGD (Dangerous Goods Declaration) preparation exempted / PAX loading possible (not just CAO) in many cases / simplified import declaration at some destination countries. Note that the MSDS and general documents (Invoice, Packing List) are still required.
