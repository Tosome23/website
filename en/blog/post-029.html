Shanghai or Ningbo —
Which Port Is Safer During Typhoon Season?

Shanghai and Ningbo both sit in China's East China region, close enough geographically that schedules and rates are usually similar. The exception is typhoon and sea-fog season from July to August, when the difference between the two ports can meaningfully affect lead time. Here's a breakdown of the basic differences between Shanghai and Ningbo, what to watch for during typhoon season, and a real case where a shipment had to be rerouted.

📍 Scope of this guide: Tosome Logis is based in Incheon, South Korea, and specializes exclusively in the China–Korea trade lane — importing cargo from China into Korea and exporting cargo from Korea to China. If your shipment moves between China and Korea via Shanghai or Ningbo, read on — for other routes we may not be able to help.

1. What's actually different between Shanghai and Ningbo?

Shanghai and Ningbo are both in China's East China region, close enough geographically that under normal conditions there's little difference in schedule or rate. Shanghai, as China's busiest port by volume, has more frequent sailings; Ningbo mainly serves as the alternative when Shanghai gets congested.

The catch is that "normal conditions" don't apply during typhoon and sea-fog season from July to August. During this window, the difference between the two ports can meaningfully affect actual lead time.

One variable the table doesn't capture: the impact of typhoon season isn't the same every time. Depending on a typhoon's path and strength, both ports can be affected simultaneously — checking the actual schedule at the time of booking matters more than the general pattern above.

3. What changes during typhoon and sea-fog season (July-August)?

July and August bring more frequent days when typhoons and sea fog restrict port operations entirely. Because Shanghai handles such high volume, a single closure can leave a backlog that takes several extra days to clear even after operations resume.

🔵 During this season, the key isn't picking "the port with more sailings normally" — it's flexibly choosing "whichever port has the more stable schedule right now."

This matters especially for time-sensitive cargo — trade show items, event goods, anything with a hard deadline. Committing to Shanghai alone and then getting hit by a typhoon can mean missing the window to switch to Ningbo as well.

4. Common problems with Shanghai vs. Ningbo shipping

What happens if you stick with Shanghai no matter what

In August 2026, a customer needed to import event items from Japan into Korea. The original plan was to route through Shanghai, but when three typhoons moved north in quick succession starting August 25, we judged that Shanghai's schedule was going to become unstable and rerouted through the Weihai ferry (car ferry) instead. As a result, the cargo made it in time for the event. As a side note, staying with Shanghai as originally planned would likely have meant about a week's delay.

Assuming Ningbo is automatically safe

Depending on a typhoon's path, both Shanghai and Ningbo can be affected at the same time. Ningbo isn't automatically the right answer — the actual typhoon path and port conditions at the time of booking need to be checked together.

Underestimating sea fog compared to typhoons

It's common to watch for typhoons and miss port restrictions caused by sea fog. In summer, sea fog can affect schedules just as much as typhoons do.
