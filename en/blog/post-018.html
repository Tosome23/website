MSDS & UN Number Documentation
Everything to Check Before You Export

Not knowing how to prepare dangerous goods paperwork stops a lot of exporters before they even start. It's widely believed that without an MSDS, export simply isn't possible — but there are solutions. Here's every document type needed for a dangerous goods export from Korea, what's inside an MSDS, how to look up a UN number, how to prepare a DGD, and the Limited Quantity exemption thresholds, laid out step by step.

2. What Is an MSDS? — The 16 Sections Explained

An MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet), also increasingly called an SDS (Safety Data Sheet), documents the hazards and safe handling information for a chemical or dangerous good. Under international standards (GHS), it's organized into 16 sections.

SectionSection NameKey Content
Sec. 1Identification of the substance and companyProduct name, manufacturer, emergency contact
Sec. 2Hazard identificationGHS classification, warning labels
Sec. 3Composition/information on ingredientsChemical composition, CAS numbers
Sec. 4-8First aid, fire, spill, handling & storageEmergency response procedures
Sec. 9Physical and chemical propertiesFlash point, boiling point, specific gravity, etc.
Sec. 14Transport informationUN number, hazard class, PI number — most important for export
Sec. 15-16Regulatory information & otherWhether export/import restrictions apply

The most important section for a dangerous goods export is Section 14 (transport information). The UN number, hazard class, packing group (PG), and PI number are all specified here.

3. What to Do If You Don't Have an MSDS

No MSDS doesn't mean you have to give up right away. Try the steps below to find a solution.

01
Request an MSDS from the manufacturer
This is the most accurate approach. Contact the manufacturer's sales representative or technical department and request an SDS/MSDS. Most manufacturers are obligated to provide one.
For overseas manufacturers, check the SDS download section of the product website Most email requests are fulfilled within 1-3 days
02
Use a similar product's MSDS
Reference a product with the same composition and hazard class, and discuss with your forwarder. Note that different composition can change the transport class, so expert review is required.
03
Check the Limited Quantity (LQ) exemption
If the shipment quantity falls under the Limited Quantity exemption threshold, dispatch as general cargo without an MSDS may be possible in some cases. Tosome Logis will check eligibility for you free of charge.
04
Have an MSDS newly prepared by a specialist agency
A domestic MSDS preparation agency can also draft one from scratch. Providing composition information (TDS, specification sheets, etc.) enables preparation.

4. What Is a UN Number? — How to Look It Up and Use It

A UN number is a four-digit code used to identify dangerous goods internationally. You need a UN number before you can prepare dangerous goods documentation.

①
Check Section 14 of the MSDS
In most cases, the UN number is listed in Section 14 (transport information) of the MSDS (SDS). This is the fastest way to confirm it.
②
Search the IATA DGR or the IMDG Code
For air, look up the substance name or chemical formula in the IATA DGR (Dangerous Goods Regulations); for sea, use the IMDG Code. Since these are specialist references, it's more practical to ask your forwarder to confirm.
③
Ask a specialist forwarder
Tell Tosome Logis the item name and composition information, and we'll look up the UN number for you free of charge.

6. Exporting Without Full Documentation — Limited Quantity Exemption

Even dangerous goods can be dispatched like general cargo, with some documentation requirements waived, if they meet the Limited Quantity (LQ) exemption threshold. Not every dangerous good qualifies for LQ treatment, though.

✅ Limited Quantity exemption criteria, summarized:
The item must meet both an inner-packaging volume threshold (varies by class) and a per-package total weight limit of 30kg or below. Attaching the LQ diamond mark to the outer packaging can allow dispatch without a DGD or MSDS in some cases. The exact thresholds vary by item, so check with your forwarder.
