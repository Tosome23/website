China–Korea Freight Forwarding Rate Structure Explained
Every hidden surcharge, at a glance

Ever gotten wildly different quotes from different China–Korea freight forwarders for the same cargo on the same route — and then found a charge on the final invoice you'd never seen before? Most of the time it comes down to how much of the surcharge stack sits on top of the base ocean freight, and how much of it each forwarder actually discloses upfront. Here's a 2026 practical breakdown of the BAF, CAF, LSS and CIC surcharges that often go missing from quotes, who pays what under each Incoterm, and the full cost structure behind freight forwarding between China and Korea.

📍 Scope of this guide: Tosome Logis is based in Incheon, South Korea, and specializes exclusively in the China–Korea trade lane — importing cargo from China into Korea and exporting cargo from Korea to China. This article does not cover freight forwarding between China and other countries. If your shipment moves between China and Korea, read on — for other routes, we likely can't help.

1. Why do China–Korea freight forwarding quotes vary so much?

Even for identical cargo on the same China–Korea route, quotes from different forwarders can vary widely. There's usually one reason: base ocean freight (O/F) is just the starting number — how much of the surcharge stack gets added on top, and how transparently it's disclosed, differs by forwarder.

It's the same structure as buying a plane ticket where the listed fare doesn't include the fuel surcharge or airport fees. If you don't understand the rate structure and simply pick the forwarder with the lowest headline freight, you can end up with unexpected charges once the cargo arrives in Korea — sometimes adding up to hundreds of dollars, occasionally over a thousand.

This guide walks through the base freight and surcharges that make up China freight forwarding rates, who's responsible for which cost under each Incoterm, and how to compare quotes accurately.

2. The basics of ocean freight — base rate and surcharges

Ocean freight on a China forwarding quote generally consists of two parts.

ComponentWhat it covers
Base rate (O/F, Ocean Freight)Core sea transport cost, priced per container or by CBM (volume)
SurchargesAdditional charges layered on top of the base rate — driven by fuel prices, currency swings, container supply-demand, etc.

Comparing forwarders on base rate alone is misleading. The China–Korea trade lane in particular carries more surcharge line items than many other routes, so a lower base rate doesn't always mean a lower total once surcharges are added.

3. Four surcharges that commonly go missing from quotes

Four surcharges show up especially often on the China–Korea lane. If a quote doesn't list any of these, treat it as a red flag that they may be missing, not included.

ChargeFull nameWhy it exists
BAFBunker Adjustment FactorOffsets carrier losses from bunker fuel price fluctuations
CAFCurrency Adjustment FactorOffsets currency exchange losses from settling freight in USD
LSSLow Sulfur SurchargeCovers the added cost of low-sulfur fuel required under environmental regulations
CIC (EIS)Container Imbalance ChargeOffsets container supply-demand imbalance on a given trade lane

These four charges aren't applied uniformly across every trade lane worldwide — whether they're charged, and how much, depends on the lane and the carrier. The China–Korea lane sees BAF, CAF and CIC especially often.

4. Who pays what under each Incoterm

In principle, which party bears these surcharges is determined by the Incoterm the shipment is contracted under.

Charge typeF-terms (EXW·FOB·FCA)C-terms (CFR·CIF)D-terms (DAP·DDP)
Freight surcharges
(BAF·CAF·LSS)		Importer paysExporter paysExporter pays
Destination charges
(CIC, etc.)		Importer paysImporter paysExporter pays

That's the theory. In practice, charges like BAF, CAF and LSS are frequently billed late by the carrier after arrival at the import country, which is a common source of disputes between exporter and importer. It's especially common for importers to get stuck with these costs even under a C-term (CFR/CIF) contract where the exporter should technically be paying.

⚠️ If your deal is on a C-term (CFR/CIF), the safest move is to agree with the exporter upfront, at the contract stage, on who covers these surcharges.

5. The full cost structure beyond ocean freight

A China forwarding quote doesn't stop at freight and surcharges. Once cargo reaches port, several more charges accumulate in sequence. Here's the typical full cost breakdown in practice.

✅ A quote that bundles all of the above into one confirmed figure upfront is commonly called an all-in quote. The alternative — a low headline freight rate with the rest billed one item at a time — can leave you with a final invoice much higher than the original quote.

6. How to compare China–Korea freight forwarding quotes accurately

Understanding the rate structure doesn't help if a quote simply omits line items with no way to verify them. Use this checklist when comparing quotes.

1
Check for BAF·CAF·LSS·CIC on the quote
Confirm first whether these four items are explicitly listed.
2
If missing, ask directly
If they're not listed, ask the forwarder directly whether they're included.
3
Confirm the all-in total
Check whether THC, D/O fee, CFS warehouse fee and inland trucking are all included in the total.
4
Confirm the Incoterm first
Lock in the Incoterm before requesting a quote so you know which costs are yours to bear.
5
Get at least two quotes
Compare the line-item structure across two or more providers. Requesting a quote is free.
