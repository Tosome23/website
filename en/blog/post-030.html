China Port Selection Strategy,
Shanghai, Ningbo, Shenzhen, Guangzhou Compared

China's geography is vast, so the port your factory should use can differ completely depending on where it's located. Sending Shanghai-area cargo through Shenzhen, or Guangzhou cargo through Shanghai, can sometimes make inland trucking cost more than the ocean freight itself. The key with China port selection strategy is that the closest port isn't always the best one — schedule frequency, rates and customs processing speed all vary by port, so the right choice depends on your factory location and cargo type. This guide compares the characteristics and lead times of Shanghai, Ningbo, Shenzhen, Guangzhou and Weihai, and when each port makes sense.

3. Which port fits your factory's location?

If your factory is in East China (Shanghai, Hangzhou, Suzhou, etc.) — Shanghai or Ningbo is the default choice. Both ports have ample schedules, so spreading cargo to Ningbo when Shanghai is congested is also an option.

If your factory is in South China (Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Dongguan, Foshan, etc.) — use Shenzhen or Guangzhou port. Items with high import volumes, like electronics and IT components, benefit from Shenzhen's proximity to industrial clusters for efficient pickup.

If your factory is in Shandong (Weihai, Qingdao) — the Weihai ferry gets you the shortest distance to Korea, arriving in as little as one to two days. That said, ferries mostly handle smaller cargo volumes, so it's worth confirming whether your cargo fits.

If your factory is in an inland city with no port (Changsha, Yiwu, etc.) — cargo needs inland trucking to the nearest port first. Since inland trucking makes up a large share of total cost in this case, you should compare distance to port and inland trucking cost together.

4. Problems that come up with China port selection

Choosing simply "the biggest port"

Shanghai having the highest cargo volume doesn't mean it's advantageous for every shipment. Routing South China factory cargo through Shanghai can add inland trucking cost, sometimes increasing total cost rather than lowering it.

Booking without checking peak-season schedules

Around Chinese New Year or typhoon season, cancellations and delays occur port by port. When cancellations pile up at one port, rerouting through an alternative (e.g. Ningbo when Shanghai is congested) can be faster.

Quoting inland-city cargo like ordinary port cargo

Inland cities like Changsha require inland trucking to a port, which is essential — but this cost sometimes gets left out of the initial quote and billed as an extra charge later.
